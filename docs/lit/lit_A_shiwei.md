## PART 2 — THE GROUP'S STANDARD PIPELINE ("the ES&T recipe")

**Recipe A — chemical-structure → receptor activity prediction (Wei Shi's signature, 2020→2026):**
1. Harvest a large curated activity dataset (ToxCast/Tox21, EPA CompTox, literature; 4000–8000+ chemicals) and **aggressively curate** it (the 2026 EAT paper removed 20.1% false positives and 41.5% false negatives in a 5-step curation).
2. **Hierarchical "characteristic fragment" decomposition** — primary fragments → secondary fragments → tertiary fragments; primary/secondary govern *binding*, tertiary governs *activity type* (agonist/antagonist/mixed). This is the group's most distinctive and most-reused idea.
3. **High-throughput molecular docking** into the receptor LBD (12 classic nuclear receptors in the 2021 paper), plus **MD** on selected complexes to rationalise the fragment effects.
4. Build a **classification or (newer) quantitative** model — fragment-based clustering then cluster-specific regression (2026), AOP-constrained ML (RepTox 2022), or graph DL on a causal KG (PNAS 2026).
5. **Validate in vitro** — TR-FRET competitive binding, or reporter-gene assays (MVLN/T47D-ARE for ER, MDA-kb2 for AR/GR), occasionally zebrafish.
6. **Apply to an inventory** — China's existing-chemical substance list, to produce a prioritised hit list (e.g. 2100 male / 7281 female reproductive toxicants).

**Recipe B — sample → effect → toxicant identification (the EDA/"mechanism-directed" line):**
1. Concentrate real samples (SPE/HLB, passive samplers, Tenax bioaccessible extraction for dust).
2. Run a **concentration-dependent reduced transcriptome** — RHT (HepG2, human), RZT (zebrafish embryo), or the newer **ERHT** (1200 endocrine genes / 110 pathways / 8 adverse outcomes) — to get pathway-level potencies and tPODs *without* pre-choosing an endpoint.
3. Let the transcriptome **nominate the toxicity mechanism**, then pick the confirmatory bioassay (zebrafish behaviour for neurotoxicity, reporter gene for endocrine).
4. **Non-target / suspect / pseudotargeted LC-HRMS** on the same extracts.
5. **Match chemistry to biology** via a bespoke relational layer: chemical–pathway interaction database (2413 interactions), **QPSR** (qualitative pathway–structure relationship), pathway "active fragments", or structure-based risk markers. Docking/in-silico is used as a *candidate filter*, not as the primary evidence.
6. Confirm the shortlisted toxicants with standards + single-compound bioassay, and report 5–10 "key toxicants" (atrazine, hexazinone, niclosamide, etc.).

**Targets worked on (verified from the corpus):** ERα, AR, GR, TR/thyroid axis, plus "12 classic nuclear receptors" (set membership not individually verified here), AhR (via Xiaowei Zhang's arm), PPARγ (via collaborators), nicotinic acetylcholine and other neuro-receptors (thiamethoxam paper).
**Pollutant classes:** bisphenol analogues; phthalates and their "safer" substitutes (DINP, DINCH, DGD, GTA); organophosphate esters / TPhP; halogenated and halophenolic **disinfection by-products** (a very large share of the group's output, with Yang Pan and Qing Zhou); PFAS (mostly via Si Wei / Nanyang Yu); short-chain chlorinated paraffins; pharmaceuticals & PPCPs; neonicotinoids; wastewater/tap-water/source-water mixtures.
**No evidence found in this corpus for:** novel brominated flame retardants as a *Wei Shi* focus (the NBFR/DBDPE paper is Zhejiang Univ.), or transthyretin binding as a signature target.

## PART 3 — HONEST ASSESSMENT OF THE COMPUTATIONAL CEILING (Wei Shi, NJU)

**What they genuinely do:**
- Rigid-receptor **molecular docking at scale** (thousands of ligands, a dozen receptors) — their real workhorse.
- **Classical MD** on selected complexes, used qualitatively/mechanistically (2020 ES&T; the 2022 Chinese review explicitly covers MD, and explicitly names docking's limitations — rigid receptor, approximate scoring, insufficient sampling — as the reason to add MD).
- **Fragment / substructure SAR** that is genuinely original and has fed into VEGA-HUB and the OECD QSAR Toolbox (per the faculty page; UNVERIFIED by a tool that returned the toolbox entry itself).
- **Shallow-to-mid ML**: RF/SVM-class classifiers, multilayer perceptrons (AOP-CTMP), cluster-specific regressors. AUCs 0.89–0.98 on internal/external splits.
- **Knowledge-graph + graph deep learning** since ~2026 (EDKG-DL, PNAS) — a real jump.
- Best-in-class **wet-lab coupling**: reduced transcriptomes, reporter-gene panels, zebrafish behaviour/development, and field campaigns (Yangtze, Taihu, plateau lakes, national dust and tap-water surveys). This is their true comparative advantage.

**What they do NOT do (no evidence in the verified corpus):**
- **No alchemical / rigorous free-energy calculations** — no FEP, no thermodynamic integration, no absolute binding free energies.
- **MM/GBSA is UNVERIFIED** for this group. It is widely used in the field and plausibly appears in SI, but no tool returned a Wei Shi paper stating MM/GBSA. Do not attribute it.
- **No enhanced-sampling MD** verified (metadynamics, umbrella sampling, aMD). One WebSearch summary claimed a 2025 ES&T group paper used "enhanced sampling molecular dynamics" for combined estrogenic/anti-estrogenic disruptors; **I could not find that record in PubMed — treat as UNVERIFIED/possibly a search hallucination.**
- **No machine-learned interatomic potentials / MLMD** (contrast: Jingwen Chen's group at DUT does exactly this for hydrolysis kinetics).
- **No AlphaFold / co-folding / structure-prediction-driven target discovery** verified.
- **No generative models** — no de novo design of safer alternatives (contrast: DUT's ParetoGen and chemical-language-model work).
- **No chemical foundation models / pretrained molecular LLMs as predictors.** They *review* AI tooling (ES&T 2026 evidence-synthesis paper) and they *embed* a KG into a DL model (PNAS 2026), but they do not fine-tune ChemBERTa-class models (contrast: Shulin Zhuang at ZJU).
- **No multi-omics integration beyond transcriptomics** (no paired proteomics/metabolomics/lipidomics in their own first-author work; metabolomics appears only on Si Wei's exposomics papers).
- Honest one-line summary: **"docking + fragment SAR + curated-data ML/AOP models, wrapped around a very strong transcriptomic + zebrafish + field-sampling experimental core — now escalating into knowledge-graph deep learning."** Not a methods-development ML lab; a toxicology lab with a competent and improving computational layer.
