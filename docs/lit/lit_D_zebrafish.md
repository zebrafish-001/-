# Literature Survey D — High-information zebrafish behavioural phenotyping as the validation layer for computational pollutant-toxicity prediction

Compiled 2026-11-13. Sources: PubMed (via MCP PubMed tool), Consensus semantic search, targeted web search.
**Verification policy used here:** every entry marked **[V]** has had its title, author list, year, journal, volume/pages and DOI confirmed from PubMed metadata. Entries marked **[P]** are partially verified (title/authors/year/journal confirmed from a Consensus record or publisher page, but DOI not independently confirmed). Entries marked **[PRE]** are preprints or reviewed preprints. Entries marked **[UNVERIFIED]** are leads only — do not cite without checking. api.openalex.org was blocked and not used.

## 0. Executive summary for the lab

1. The behavioural-fingerprinting field splits cleanly into two lineages. **Lineage A (pharmacology/UCSF–MGH: Kokel, Peterson, Keiser, Rihel/Schier)** uses short, stimulus-dense photomotor batteries on thousands of compounds and maps phenotype→target by *profile similarity* to reference drugs, then validates with radioligand binding or mutants. **Lineage B (regulatory tox: EPA/Padilla, Tanguay, UFZ/Tal, NIEHS DTT, Biobide/ZeClinics)** uses the light–dark transition test (LDTT) / larval photomotor response (LPR) on tens-to-hundreds of chemicals and maps phenotype→hazard call, with potency (BMC/POD) as the deliverable. The lab's planned engine sits naturally between them and the biggest unclaimed ground is *Lineage A's resolution applied to Lineage B's chemicals*.
2. **The single most useful paper for the lab's task 4 question is Herold et al. 2025 (Neurotoxicology)** — 26 endpoints, 63 reference chemicals, 5,159 control larvae, and an explicit mapping of receptor class → behavioural fingerprint. It is the closest thing to a published look-up table of "which endpoint discriminates which target."
3. **Habituation of the acoustic/visual startle response, measured at high frame rate with per-event C-start classification, is the sharpest readout in the larval repertoire.** PPI is the second sharpest and is the only one with a demonstrated dopaminergic-vs-glutamatergic double dissociation. Dark-phase total distance is the bluntest — it is where almost all the published false-positive/direction-flip problems live.
4. Colín-García et al. 2026 is a direct, controlled demonstration that **the lab's high-speed camera purchase doubles mechanism-specificity**: 1000 fps kinematic classification reproduced the expected direction of effect for 6/8 reference neuroactives vs 3/8 for a 30 fps centroid workflow, on larvae from the same spawning and shared stocks.
5. Unsupervised motif discovery (B-SOiD / VAME / keypoint-MoSeq) has **essentially not been applied to zebrafish chemical screening** — this is a real, verified gap and a defensible novelty claim, but note the methodological warning from Gendelev et al. 2024 about shortcut learning.
6. Loop closures already published (do not duplicate): PFOS→GABA-A/B, PFOS/PFHxS→PPARδ, carbaryl→ADRA2B+HTR2B, chlorophene→GABA-A/Kv7, σ1→threat switch, TPO/DIO→swim-bladder inflation, PFAS→folate receptor β, hcrtr2 antagonists, MDPV cardiac AV-block vs behaviour.
7. Practical power numbers now exist: **8–32 larvae/dose for 80% power at 20% effect** on the best LDTT endpoints, rising to **182–650** on the worst; expect **3.2–3.5-fold** between-lab potency spread; budget to discard ~**11%** of plates on QC.

## 4. Which assays give mechanism-specific signatures (the sharp-vs-blunt question)

### 4.1 The published look-up table — Herold et al. 2025 (VAMR, 26 endpoints, 63 reference chemicals, 5,159 control larvae)

Verbatim from the abstract (DOI 10.1016/j.neuro.2025.05.001):

| Behavioural signature | Target classes producing it |
|---|---|
| **Reduced habituation learning** | NMDA receptor; GABA-A receptor |
| **Inappropriately *accelerated* habituation learning** | PPARδ; PPARγ; GABA-A; dopamine; **ryanodine receptor**; AhR; GPCRs; SRC tyrosine kinase |
| **Inappropriate hyperactivity** | GABA-A; NMDA; dopamine; PPARα; PPARδ; EGF; BMP; AhR; retinoid X; **α2-adrenoceptor** |
| **Paradoxical excitation = dark-phase sedation + INCREASED startle response** | **GABA-A receptor antagonists** |
| Same endpoints with **opposite directionality** | **GABA-A/B receptor agonists** |

Read the structure, not just the rows:
- **Hyperactivity is the least informative signature** — 10 different target classes produce it.
- **Habituation *direction* splits the target space**: NMDA/GABA-A reduce it; PPARδ/γ, dopamine, RyR, AhR, GPCR, SRC accelerate it. Acceleration is the rarer, more diagnostic direction.
- **The composite "dark-phase sedation + increased startle" is specific enough to call GABA-A antagonism, and its sign flips cleanly for agonism.** That bidirectional sign-flip is what makes it a sharp test rather than a correlation.
- Environmental GABA-A antagonists lindane and dieldrin, and the agonist **TBBPA**, all produced the predicted fingerprints — the mapping transfers from drugs to pollutants.
- **The learning/memory endpoints carry unique information** — the 5,159-control-larvae correlation analysis showed they are not redundant with locomotion. This is the formal justification for the lab's richer engine.

### 4.2 Startle habituation measured at high frame rate — the sharpest single readout, and the frame-rate evidence

**Colín-García K, Tagkalidou N, Aljabasini O, Prats E, Barata C, Gómez-Oliván LM, Raldúa D.** Zebrafish startle habituation: how readout choice influences the interpretation of chemical effects in neurotoxicity screening. *Ecotoxicol Environ Saf* 2026;319:120289. DOI 10.1016/j.ecoenv.2026.120289 (PMID 42161110) **[V]**

Controlled cross-platform comparison, **embryos from the same spawning and shared stock solutions**, 8 neuroactive reference chemicals, 5 dpf:

| Workflow | Metric | Directional concordance with expected modulation |
|---|---|---|
| Centroid-based, **30 fps**, post-stimulus displacement AUC | AUC | **3/8 compounds** |
| High-speed kinematic, **1000 fps**, per-event C-start classification | % habituation | **6/8 compounds** |

Additional findings with direct operational consequences:
- The 30 fps centroid workflow showed **low sensitivity to ketamine despite ketamine markedly changing habituation kinetics** — i.e. centroid tracking can miss a true NMDA-receptor effect entirely.
- **Nimodipine showed that both assays are needed**: without the kinematic channel, a complete loss of the motor response is misread as "altered plasticity". **This is the single most important trap for the lab's engine — a drop in habituation score can mean the fish cannot move, not that it cannot learn. Always report startle *responsiveness* alongside habituation.**
- The high-speed workflow "provided detailed resolution of C-start mechanics".

**Conclusion for the lab: the high-speed camera roughly doubles mechanism-specificity on the habituation endpoint. This is the strongest single justification for that purchase in the literature.**

### 4.4 Habituation pharmacology — mechanistic anchors

| Finding | Citation |
|---|---|
| **Two temporally distinguishable forms of C-start habituation with different cellular mechanisms: the short-term form (≥25 min, <1 h, spaced training) is NMDAR-dependent; the rapid form (≥1 min, <15 min, 120 pulses at 0.5–2 Hz) is NOT.** The lab's protocol choice therefore determines which receptor it is testing. | **Roberts AC et al.** Habituation of the C-start response in larval zebrafish exhibits several distinct phases and sensitivity to NMDA receptor blockade. *PLoS ONE* 2011. **[P]** |
| Larval habituation satisfies the formal criteria (spontaneous recovery, ISI dependence, dishabituation). Rolipram (PDE4), donepezil (AChE inhibitor) and memantine (NMDA antagonist) all **increased startle and decreased habituation**. **The memantine effect was blocked by NMDA; the donepezil effect was blocked by mecamylamine but NOT atropine → nicotinic, not muscarinic.** | **Best JD et al.** Non-associative learning in larval zebrafish. *Neuropsychopharmacology* 2007. **[P]** — **the mecamylamine/atropine dissociation is an off-the-shelf sharp test for a predicted nAChR mechanism and has apparently never been applied to a pollutant.** |
| Visual (dark-flash) habituation: Ca²⁺ imaging found **12 functional neuron classes** differing in adaptation rate, response shape and anatomy, distributed across the brain. Small-molecule screening implicated melatonin, oestrogen and GABA signalling. **The authors explicitly failed to find a simple relationship between pharmacology, activity patterns and habituation behaviour** and conclude habituation is a complex distributed plasticity process not captured by a simple model. | **Lamiré L-A et al.** Functional and pharmacological analyses of visual habituation learning in larval zebrafish. *eLife* 2023. **[P]** — **the necessary caution: habituation is mechanistically sharp for NMDA/GABA-A, but it is not a one-pathway readout.** |
| Only the **SLC** (Mauthner-mediated short-latency C-bend) is susceptible to PPI; LLC kinematics are unchanged after bilateral Mauthner ablation; SLC responsiveness declines much faster than LLC under rapid stimulus trains; across >180 larvae **SLC kinematics (latency, duration, turning angle, angular velocity) were identical at all phases of a habituation assay** — i.e. habituation changes SLC *probability*, not SLC *form*. | Reviews/primary work surfaced via search **[UNVERIFIED in detail — confirm against Burgess & Granato 2007 full text and Tabor et al. *Curr Biol* 2014 before citing]** — **but the operational consequence is firm and important: the lab should score habituation as SLC probability, and treat a change in SLC kinematics as a different (motor/Mauthner-level) finding.** |
| Light-stimulus *intensity* positively predicts locomotor responses via larger startle-habituation decay constants and offsets. | **Beppi C et al.** Light-stimulus intensity modulates startle reflex habituation in larval zebrafish. *Sci Rep* 2021. **[P]** — stimulus intensity is a confounder that must be fixed and reported. |
| Touch-induced C-start habituation shows **input specificity**. | **Roberts AC et al.** *PLoS ONE* 2019. **[P]** — input specificity is a built-in internal control: a generic sick fish habituates everywhere. |

### 4.5 Thigmotaxis — more sensitive than locomotion, and partly orthogonal to it

Torres-Ruiz et al. 2026 **[P]**: BMD modelling showed thigmotaxis was **generally more sensitive than traditional locomotor endpoints** across compounds; caffeine ↑, diazepam ↓ under specific stimulus conditions; saccharin and amoxicillin (low-neuroactivity comparators) gave little or no effect; 24-round-well and 96-square-well formats equivalent. The companion 37-substance screen reported **9/37 substances with thigmotaxis-specific anxiety-like signals not explained by locomotion alone**, and 8/37 with mixed or stimulus-dependent (including non-monotonic) patterns. **Sharp for anxiogenic/anxiolytic axis; blunt for receptor identity.**

### 4.6 Spontaneous tail coiling / touch-evoked response / AChE — the cautionary case

This is where the lab should be most sceptical. Verified evidence of direction instability:

- AChE-inhibiting compounds can **raise** coiling at low concentrations and **reduce** it at higher ones (**[P]**, 2023 coiling-sensitivity study, *Environ Sci Pollut Res*).
- Organophosphates produce **hypoactivity in the touch-evoked response but hyperactivity in spontaneous coiling at 24 hpf** — attributed to exposure duration/concentration and to coiling reflecting primary rather than secondary motoneurons. "Evaluation of the effects of acetylcholinesterase inhibitors in the zebrafish touch-evoked response: quantitative vs. qualitative assessment", *Environ Sci Eur* 2020, DOI 10.1186/s12302-020-00421-7 **[P]**.
- Measurements near 26 hpf are confounded because larvae still show some spontaneous coiling.
- Spontaneous tail contractions at 26 hpf are sensitive to paraoxon **possibly independently of AChE inhibition** (**[P]**, 2022).
- The spontaneous tail coiling test "discriminates activity changes by mode of action when they are hyper- or hypoactivity, **but not between different mechanisms producing the same direction**" (**[P]**).
- Assay-to-MoA matching is itself direction-dependent: **STC better predicts organophosphate hyperactivity; PMR and LMR-L/D better predict anticonvulsant hypoactivity** (**[P]**).
- **Carbaryl's behavioural effects at environmental concentrations were AChE-independent** and were instead ADRA2B/HTR2B-mediated (Faria 2022, **[V]**).

**Verdict: coiling and touch-response are BLUNT for AChE. A behavioural change in a predicted AChE inhibitor is weak evidence; it must be paired with direct AChE activity measurement. Conversely, "behaviour changed and it's a carbamate so it's AChE" is exactly the inference Faria 2022 disproved.**

### 4.7 PTZ seizure model — sharp for proconvulsant liability, blunt for mechanism

- 7 dpf larvae exposed to the GABA-A antagonist PTZ show increased locomotor activity, seizure-like behaviour and epileptiform electrographic activity; the locomotor assay is "useful as a rapid first-pass screening tool", but behavioural and electrographic responses to antiepileptics needed separate validation (**Afrikanova T et al.**, *PLoS ONE* 2013, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0054166 **[P]**).
- A systematic review found PTZ in **180 of 201** included zebrafish seizure studies, with kainic acid and pilocarpine far rarer — **so comparative mechanism data barely exist** (**[PRE]** bioRxiv 2023).
- A 2025 study combining movement tracking with calcium fluorescence and ML for low-replicate anti-seizure screening suggests movement alone is not the only discriminating signal (**[P]**, *Eur J Pharmacol*).
- **No evidence was found that the locomotor burst pattern discriminates convulsant mechanisms.**

**Verdict: a PTZ-model result confirms "proconvulsant / GABAergic disinhibition liability", not "GABA-A receptor binding". For receptor-level specificity use the Herold-style paradoxical-excitation fingerprint (dark-phase sedation + increased startle) with its agonist/antagonist sign flip, which *is* bidirectional, plus a picrotoxin/propofol rescue in the Owen 2025 design.**

### 4.8 Cardiac readouts — hERG/QT

- In a 100-compound screen, drugs causing human QT prolongation "consistently caused bradycardia and AV block in the zebrafish"; a transgenic myocardial-GFP larval model reported **25/30** human QT-prolonging compounds and **21/26** hERG blockers caused bradycardia (**[P]**, Wen et al. *J Appl Toxicol* 2012 and related).
- The orthologue is **zERG**; hERG blockade in larvae can be detected without an ECG because it produces bradycardia or **2:1 atrioventricular block / AV decoupling** (**[P]**).
- **Specificity limits:** of 23 known I_Kr blockers, 18 caused bradycardia; the 5 that did not (erythromycin, NAPA, pentamidine, procainamide, sotalol) did so once the absorption barrier was bypassed — i.e. **larval bioavailability, not pharmacology, limits sensitivity**. Pindolol, not a human QTc prolonger, increased zebrafish QTc — a likely false positive (**[P]**).
- **MDPV (verified, [V]):** AV-block EC50 228 µM vs locomotor EC50 2.51 µM, with cardiac output down at all concentrations but fractional shortening and stroke volume unchanged, and repolarising K⁺ channel genes unaltered — **a worked example of quantitatively separating a chronotropic/conduction phenotype from a contractile one and from behaviour.**

**Verdict: bradycardia alone is BLUNT. The sharp cardiac readouts are (i) the AV-conduction ratio / 2:1 block, which is comparatively specific to repolarisation-channel block, and (ii) the chronotropic-vs-contractile decomposition (heart rate and AV ratio vs ejection fraction, fractional shortening, stroke volume, cardiac output) that the lab's Vevo Doppler/ECG stack can deliver far better than video-based labs. Always report the behaviour-to-cardiac EC50 ratio.**

### 4.9 Swim bladder / thyroid

Sharp at the organ level, weak at the behavioural level. Anterior-vs-posterior chamber dissociation is mechanistically informative (maternal T4 depletion by 32 dpf explains why TPO inhibition only hits the anterior chamber late) and **DIO in chemico data predicted posterior-chamber effects in 14 compounds with only 2 outliers** (Stinckens 2018 **[V]**). But **AOP-Wiki AOP 159 rates inflation → reduced swimming performance only moderate-to-low confidence**, and the mechanism linking TH disruption to impaired inflation remains unknown. **So: swim-bladder inflation is a sharp test of a predicted TPO/DIO MIE; swimming performance is currently a blunt one — and closing that specific gap is an open, well-defined contribution.**

### 4.10 Summary ranking — sharp to blunt

| Rank | Readout | Sharpness | Key constraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Startle habituation, high-speed per-event C-start classification (SLC probability), with startle responsiveness reported alongside** | Sharpest. Direction (reduced vs accelerated) splits target space; 6/8 vs 3/8 concordance advantage over centroid | Needs ≥~120–1000 fps; must separate "cannot learn" from "cannot move" |
| 2 | **PPI, ideally with a haloperidol reversal arm** | Double dissociation: apomorphine selectively impairs PPI (haloperidol-reversible); ketamine impairs PPI *and* habituation; *Ophelia* mutant proves PPI ≠ acuity or startle performance | D2 reversal only worked at 7 dpf, not 5–6 dpf |
| 3 | **Composite GABA-A fingerprint: dark-phase sedation + increased startle, with sign flip for agonists** | Bidirectional, validated on lindane/dieldrin/TBBPA | Needs both light-dark and startle channels in one run |
| 4 | **Cardiac AV-conduction ratio + chronotropic/contractile decomposition (Doppler)** | AV block comparatively specific to repolarisation-channel block | Bioavailability-limited false negatives; pindolol-type false positives |
| 5 | **Swim bladder inflation, anterior vs posterior, with stage control** | Sharp for TPO/DIO MIE | Only detectable late (≈32 dpf) for TPO; weak link to swimming |
| 6 | **Thigmotaxis (visual + acoustic challenge)** | More sensitive than locomotion; partly orthogonal | Sharp on anxiety axis only, not receptor identity |
| 7 | **Visual startle response (VSR) hyperactivity, developmental vs acute** | Developmental-only, irreversible VSR hyperactivity was the phenotype that isolated ppard; acute-vs-developmental contrast is itself diagnostic | Needs washout design |
| 8 | **Dark-phase / total distance moved** | Bluntest. ≥10 target classes produce hyperactivity; direction flips with concentration, stage, acclimation, well geometry, plate colour, temperature, light intensity | Never use alone as mechanism evidence |
| 9 | **Spontaneous coiling / touch-evoked response for AChE** | Blunt and direction-unstable | Must be paired with AChE activity assay |
| 10 | **PTZ locomotor burst for GABA-A** | Confirms proconvulsant liability, not receptor binding | No published mechanism discrimination |

## 5. Reproducibility, variability and power

### 5.1 Inter-laboratory

**Truong L et al.** *Neurotoxicology* 2026;116:103504. DOI 10.1016/j.neuro.2026.103504 (PMID 42392410) **[V]** — 4 labs, 33 compounds, harmonised LDTT:

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Developmental-toxicity endpoint concordance across labs | **84–100%** |
| **DNT activity-call concordance across labs** | **68–84%** |
| Potency (POD) reproducibility, DNT | mean **3.2-fold** |
| Potency reproducibility, DT | mean **3.5-fold** |
| Plates removed by the three-tiered QC framework, over and above basic viability criteria | **10.9%** |
| Zebrafish LDTT sensitivity / specificity (DNT-positives / DNT-negatives) | **93% / 100%** |
| DNT in vitro battery (13 assays) for comparison | **86% sensitivity / 60% specificity** |

**Operational reading: activity calls are only ~68–84% reproducible even with an agreed protocol, but potency is reproducible to ~3-fold. Report PODs/BMCs, not binary calls.**

### 5.2 Power and sample size

**Konrad KS, Allen-Moyer K, Ellis L, Hessel E, Jaka O, Muriana A, Martínez BM, Del Pozo A, Schiavone V, Di Donato V, Sanz CM, Truong L, Tanguay R, Shockley KR, Ryan K, Hsieh J-H.** Power calculations for larval zebrafish in light-dark transition test for developmental neurotoxicity. *Neurotoxicology* 2025;112:103371. DOI 10.1016/j.neuro.2025.103371 (PMID 41448279) **[V]**

- Data from **4 laboratories**, standard protocol: each 96-well plate = **5–7 test concentrations + 12–16 vehicle-control wells, 1 larva/well**.
- Power computed for t-test, ANOVA and **RMANOVA** on 4 endpoints: Total Distance, Movement Similarity, Distance Change, Distance Shift.
- **Highest power: Movement Similarity and Distance Change** — these had the lowest intra- *and* inter-laboratory variability.
- **Using those endpoints MORE THAN DOUBLED the power of the statistical tests relative to Total Distance at the same sample size, and typically required 8–32 larvae per dose group for 80% power at a 20% effect size.**
- **Distance Shift required 182–650 larvae per dose group** for a t-test at 80% power.
- **Normalising to the vehicle control on each plate minimised inter-laboratory variation.**
- Conclusion: endpoint selection, data transformation and choice of statistical test matter as much as n.

Other power numbers found:
- Larval diving response study: G*Power on pilot data → **18 larvae per condition** at 80% power, but from an implausibly large pilot effect size (η²p = 0.95) — **a worked example of pilot-derived power estimates being over-optimistic** (**[PRE]** bioRxiv 2022.05.16.492196).
- Standardised 4 dpf light/dark protocol: **n = 16 per concentration for preliminary power calculations**, then re-estimate from observed power (**[P]** Z-Loc 4 protocol).
- Experimental-parameter study used **73–192 larvae per group** to resolve plate/geometry/temperature effects (**[V]**, below).

### 5.4 Ring tests and formal guidance — what exists and what does not

- **A formal OECD-style ring test / validation study for larval zebrafish behaviour was NOT found.** Truong et al. 2026 (4 labs, 33 compounds) is the closest thing and is best described as a multi-laboratory evaluation, not a validated ring test.
- **OECD DNT In Vitro Battery Initial Recommendations, ENV/CBC/MONO(2023)13** (June 2023) include IATA proof-of-concept case studies; the OECD expert-group design is for an in vitro battery "complemented by alternative species (e.g. zebrafish) assays", and a 2024 OECD workshop abstract describes an IATA case study built from DNT-IVB plus zebrafish embryo behavioural data. **[P] — details from secondary sources; confirm scope against ENV/CBC/MONO(2023)13 directly.**
- A recent review argues **OECD TG 426 remains the scientifically preferred approach** and that DNT in vitro batteries and zebrafish models "are not yet sufficiently validated to replace in vivo developmental neurotoxicity testing" **[P]**. The lab should expect this objection.

### 5.5 Recommended design, assembled from the verified numbers

1. **n = 24 larvae/concentration** as default for primary screening (covers 8–32 for 80% power at 20% effect on the good endpoints; matches Jarema's 16–24 and Torres-Ruiz's 24).
2. **n = 48** for any dedicated loop-closure/rescue experiment — rescue designs need to detect a *restoration*, i.e. two contrasts.
3. **Do not plan on Distance-Shift-type endpoints** unless prepared for hundreds of animals.
4. **One larva per well; 12–16 vehicle-control wells per plate; normalise to within-plate vehicle control.**
5. **Randomise treatment to well position physically, and record the randomisation**, per Gendelev 2024. Then test for position dependence in the control data as a standing QC metric.
6. **Fix and report**: strain, holding temperature, plate material/colour/opacity, well geometry and area, light intensity, acclimation duration, transition length, time of day, dpf, hatching time window. Report them against the Hill 2023 51-variable checklist.
7. **Adopt a three-tier plate QC gate and expect to discard ~11% of plates.**
8. **Run ≥3 independent clutches/batches** per conclusion and model batch as a random effect; between-batch variability is "considerable" in every verified source.
9. **Report PODs/BMCs** (via `bmdrc`) rather than binary active/inactive calls, with the Rowson 2025 bidirectional and biphasic models.
10. **Always report startle responsiveness next to habituation**, per the nimodipine result.

## 6. Strong vs weak validation designs — direct guidance

### Would be STRONG evidence for a computational target prediction

| Design | Why it is strong | Precedent |
|---|---|---|
| **Predicted target knocked down (CRISPR crispant) or null mutant, with the behavioural phenotype abolished, AND a paralogue/subtype knocked down as a negative control** | Rules out general toxicity and off-target; subtype selectivity is the discriminating evidence | Gutsfeld 2024 (ppard vs pparaa/ab vs pparg) **[V]**; Ginsparg 2026 (hcrtr2 null) **[P]**; Rennekamp 2016 (σ1 mutant) **[V]** |
| **Bidirectional pharmacology with a specific rescue**: pollutant phenocopies the antagonist, and a PAM/agonist at the *same* receptor restores baseline | A sign-flip around one receptor is very hard to produce by generic toxicity | Owen 2025 (picrotoxin phenocopy; propofol and CGP13501 rescue) **[V]**; Faria 2021 (opposite-pharmacodynamic co-exposure recovery) **[P]** |
| **Fingerprint anti-correlation**: find the co-treatment whose multi-endpoint barcode is the negative of the pollutant's, then show it rescues | Uses the lab's richest data as the search space; already proven to work | Hoffman 2016 (biochanin A vs cntnap2) **[V]** |
| **The habituation × PPI 2×2, scored at high frame rate, plus a D2-antagonist reversal arm** | Only published design with a dopaminergic/glutamatergic double dissociation | Tagkalidou 2025 **[V]**; Burgess & Granato 2007 **[V]** |
| **GABA-A sign-flip fingerprint**: dark-phase sedation + increased startle for antagonists, inverted for agonists | Bidirectional and already validated on pollutants (lindane, dieldrin, TBBPA) | Herold 2025 **[V]** |
| **mecamylamine-vs-atropine dissociation** for a predicted nAChR mechanism | Separates nicotinic from muscarinic within the cholinergic system; off-the-shelf and unused on pollutants | Best 2007 **[P]** |
| **Ion-specific rescue with a matched control ion** (e.g. Ca²⁺ rescues, equimolar Mg²⁺ does not) | The negative control ion is what makes it specific | Shahriar 2026 **[PRE]** |
| **Behaviour EC50 vs cardiac/mortality EC50 separation of ≥1–2 orders of magnitude** | Quantitatively excludes "the fish is just sick" | Aljabasini 2025 (2.51 µM vs 228 µM) **[V]** |
| **Developmental-vs-acute exposure with washout**, showing an irreversible endpoint-specific phenotype | Separates a developmental mechanism from acute pharmacology, and was the contrast that isolated ppard | Gutsfeld 2024 **[V]** |
| **Orthogonal cross-species confirmation** of the same molecular event (mouse cortical neuron patch clamp; human iPSC BrainSphere networks) | Converts a fish finding into a human-relevant mechanism claim | Owen 2025 **[V]**; Leuthold 2026 **[V]** |
| **Cardiac chronotropic/conduction vs contractile decomposition by Doppler** (heart rate + AV ratio vs EF/FS/SV/CO) with repolarising-channel transcripts measured | Discriminates a channel mechanism from pump failure; the lab's Vevo is a genuine comparative advantage here | Aljabasini 2025 **[V]** |
| **Prospective in vitro binding of behaviourally nominated compounds against the predicted human target** | The only way to claim the behavioural model predicts human target engagement; published hit rate 58% | Gendelev 2024 **[V]** |

### Would be WEAK evidence

| Design | Why it is weak |
|---|---|
| **Dark-phase or total distance moved changed → "confirms predicted target X"** | ≥10 target classes produce hyperactivity (Herold 2025); direction flips with concentration, stage, acclimation, well geometry, plate colour, temperature and light intensity (McKay 2026; Ogungbemi 2019; Hillman 2024) |
| **Docking score + "behaviour changed"** | Exactly the DBDPE→ChAT level of evidence (Hua 2022), which the authors themselves flagged as needing further investigation |
| **Network toxicology / omics enrichment + behavioural change, no causal manipulation** | The dominant pattern in the 2022–2026 pollutant literature and uniformly ★ grade; Xia 2026 **[V]** shows omics→behavioural POD correlation is real but only R² ≈ 0.54–0.64 and 8/14 within one order of magnitude externally |
| **Broad-spectrum antioxidant (quercetin, NAC) rescue** | Rescues oxidative-stress consequences of almost anything; says nothing about a receptor prediction |
| **PTZ seizure susceptibility → "GABA-A receptor binding"** | No published evidence the locomotor burst discriminates convulsant mechanism; PTZ is 180/201 of the literature so there is no comparator |
| **Spontaneous coiling or touch-response change → "AChE inhibition"** | Direction-unstable, stage-sensitive, and demonstrably dissociable from AChE (Faria 2022; paraoxon coiling) |
| **Bradycardia alone → "hERG block"** | 18/23 I_Kr blockers caused it only once absorption was bypassed; pindolol false positive; 2:1 AV block is the more specific sign |
| **Habituation score drop with no responsiveness data** | Can be a complete loss of the motor response misread as a plasticity deficit (nimodipine, Colín-García 2026 **[V]**) |
| **A novel unsupervised motif count presented as a biological fact** | Cluster number is method-determined: B-SOiD 12 vs keypoint-MoSeq 24 on identical videos (*Patterns* 2025) |
| **Any ML result from non-randomised plate layouts** | Gendelev 2024 had to re-run an entire 650-compound screen for exactly this reason |
| **Swim-bladder inflation → "reduced swimming performance" as a chain** | AOP-Wiki AOP 159 rates that specific link moderate-to-low confidence |
| **Single-clutch, single-plate, single-time-of-day experiments** | Between-batch variability is "considerable" in every verified source; diel hatching time alone shifts 5 dpf dark-phase response |

## 7. Specific openings for this lab

1. **Finish the NBFR cholinergic loop properly.** DBDPE→ChAT is docking + enzyme activity only (Hua 2022 **[V]**). The lab can add: a ChAT or nAChR crispant with a paralogue control; the mecamylamine-vs-atropine dissociation (Best 2007); high-speed C-start habituation with responsiveness reported; and a behaviour-vs-cardiac EC50 ratio. That converts a ★ to a ★★★★.
2. **Close the thyroid AOP's behavioural arm.** AOP 159's inflation→swimming-performance link is explicitly moderate-to-low confidence. The lab has swim-bladder scoring, organ histopathology quantification, and bout-level kinematics — the exact combination needed to quantify swimming performance as a function of chamber inflation, with TPO/DIO reference chemicals (MBT, methimazole, iopanoic acid, propylthiouracil) as positive controls and the anterior/posterior + stage dissociation as the internal specificity control. NBFRs are prime thyroid-disruption candidates.
3. **Bring unsupervised motif discovery to chemical screening.** Verified gap: no peer-reviewed B-SOiD/VAME/keypoint-MoSeq application to zebrafish toxicology. Report discovered motifs *against* the Marques 13-bout taxonomy so the result is interpretable and comparable, state the cluster-count caveat, and randomise well position physically from day one.
4. **Train the Green 2024 "autoencoder on controls" model on the lab's own control library.** It needs only controls, which the lab will have in abundance, and it found actives that distance-moved missed.
5. **Build the engine's output schema around three existing standards rather than inventing one:** the Hill 2023 51-variable reporting checklist, the INTOB controlled vocabulary, and the Rowson 2025 13-endpoint LMR L/D definitions with `bmdrc` for BMD fitting. This is the cheapest available route to the cross-lab comparability that every verified reproducibility paper identifies as the field's bottleneck.
6. **Exploit the Vevo.** Doppler-grade chronotropic/conduction-vs-contractile decomposition of pollutant cardiotoxicity is nearly absent from the environmental literature; MDPV (Aljabasini 2025) is the template, and the behaviour-to-cardiac EC50 ratio doubles as the lab's standing specificity control for every behavioural claim.
7. **Serotonergic, glucocorticoid, σ1 and ryanodine-receptor pollutant predictions are all unclaimed** and all have published reference pharmacology or reference fingerprints ready to use.
